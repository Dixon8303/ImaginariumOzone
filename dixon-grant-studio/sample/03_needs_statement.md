# Needs Statement — Rise & Read Watts

*(Mapped matrix rows: N6, N8, E2)*

At Site A and Site B — two Title I elementary schools in the Watts/
Willowbrook corridor of South Los Angeles — 71% of incoming
kindergarten-through-grade-5 students entered the current school year
reading below grade-level benchmark, and district-published data for the
surrounding attendance area show third-grade English Language Arts
proficiency at 24%, less than half the statewide average. Both sites
qualify as Title I schoolwide programs, with free-or-reduced-price meal
eligibility above 85%.

The gap is not a lack of instructional time in the classroom — it is a
lack of small-group, skill-matched practice time outside it. A classroom
teacher managing 28 students across a range of reading levels cannot
deliver the 90 minutes of one-on-five, phonics-matched guided reading
practice that a student reading 1.5 grade levels behind needs each week
to close the gap before it compounds into a fourth-grade reading crisis.
Every year that gap remains unaddressed, the research on early literacy
is unambiguous: a student not reading at grade level by the end of third
grade is four times less likely to graduate high school on time.

Families at both sites want this support and cannot buy it privately.
Median household income in the surrounding zip codes is less than half
the Los Angeles County median, and private tutoring — the intervention
higher-income families use to close the same gap — is priced out of
reach for nearly every family these two schools serve.

Rise & Read Watts exists to close exactly this gap: structured,
small-group literacy instruction, delivered by trained tutors, at no cost
to families, on the school campus, immediately after the instructional
day ends — reaching the 120 students at these two sites whose reading
level puts them furthest from grade-level benchmark and whose families
have the fewest alternative options to close that gap on their own.
