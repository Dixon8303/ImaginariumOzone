# 03 — Drafting: Section by Section

Purpose: turn the requirements matrix (`02_extract.md`) and intake
materials (`01_intake.md`) into narrative, budget, and supporting
documents — each section written against specific matrix rows, in a
consistent voice, with nothing invented.

## Voice

**Concrete, evidence-led, no filler.** Every claim in every section
should be one of: a number, a named fact, or a direct consequence of a
number or fact just stated. If a sentence could be cut without losing
information, cut it. Specifically:

- Lead with the specific, not the general. "120 students at two Title I
  elementary sites in Watts read below grade level" beats "many students
  in our community struggle with literacy."
- Every claim about need, capacity, or outcomes should be traceable to an
  intake document (990, program data, prior evaluation). If it can't be
  sourced, either get the source or don't write the claim.
- No throat-clearing. Never open a section with "In today's society..."
  or "It is well known that...". Open with the fact that matters most.
- No adjectives doing the work a number should do. Not "significant
  improvement" — "43% to 71% proficiency in one program year."
- Write to the rubric's language. If the funder's criterion says
  "evidence-based," use that phrase and then show the evidence. Mirroring
  the funder's own vocabulary is not padding — it is how a reviewer
  scoring against a rubric confirms a box is checked.
- Every section ends where its word/page limit ends, not before out of
  caution and not after by requesting an exception.

## Section-by-section mapping

Each section below is drafted **only** from its mapped matrix rows
(column "Mapped section" in `02_extract.md`). Cross-check the matrix
before writing each section, and again after, to confirm no row was
skipped.

### 1. Needs Statement
Mapped to: all "need"/"problem statement" narrative rows.
Answer, with data: who is affected, how badly, where, and why existing
resources don't already solve it. End by naming the specific gap this
program fills — this sentence should almost be quotable back in the
Program Narrative's opening line.

### 2. Program Narrative
Mapped to: all "program design"/"activities"/"target population" rows.
Describe what actually happens: activities, schedule, staffing, who is
served and how they're selected, and how this design responds directly
to the needs statement. Every activity described here should also appear
as an "activity" in the logic model — no orphan activities in either
direction.

### 3. Logic Model
Mapped to: any explicit logic-model requirement, plus implied
consistency with program narrative and evaluation rows.
One page: Inputs → Activities → Outputs → Outcomes (short/medium/long-
term) → Impact. Every activity in the program narrative appears here;
every outcome claimed here is measured in the evaluation plan. No
number appears in the logic model that contradicts the budget or the
program narrative.

### 4. Budget + Budget Narrative
Mapped to: all budget-form and budget-justification rows.
Line-item budget matching the funder's required categories exactly (not
the organization's internal chart of accounts). Budget narrative
justifies every line by tying it back to a specific activity in the
program narrative — a reviewer should be able to read one line item and
find the activity it funds without searching.

### 5. Evaluation Plan
Mapped to: all "evaluation"/"outcomes measurement"/"data" rows.
For every outcome in the logic model: what's measured, what instrument
or method, who collects it, how often, and how results are used to
adjust the program. No outcome in the logic model without a matching
measurement here, and no measurement here without a matching outcome
there.

### 6. Organizational Capacity Statement
Mapped to: all "organizational history"/"past performance"/"staff
qualifications" rows.
Track record (years operating, populations served, prior results),
staffing (credentials and relevant experience of the people who will
actually run this program), and fiscal/administrative capacity to manage
the award size requested. Named individuals, named results — no
unattributed "our experienced team."

### 7. RFP Compliance Checklist
Mapped to: all eligibility, attachment, and formatting rows.
One-page checklist, matrix row by matrix row, each marked complete with
its location in the package. This is the reviewer-facing proof that
nothing was missed — see `05_package.md` for the delivery format.

## Discipline rule

If a fact would make a section stronger but isn't in the intake
materials, the drafter flags it as an open question back to the client —
it is never invented, estimated without a stated basis, or left as a
vague placeholder in the delivered draft. Every number in a final
package must be traceable to a source in `01_intake.md`.
