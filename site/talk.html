TALKS FROM THE BOOK

The Price of Gold in Cairo

Mansa Musa is remembered for one line about his wealth. The Egyptian official al-Umari recorded something more exact: before the 1324 visit a mithqal of gold did not go below 25 dirhams in Cairo, and afterward it was 22 or less. The 1375 Catalan Atlas placed Musa at the center of West Africa, named and titled.

CASE FILE 01 · PP. 23, 26, 27

The Second Name on the Cover

NASA Technical Note D-233, September 1960, carries the names T. H. Skopinski and Katherine G. Johnson. By NASA's own account, it was the first time a woman in her division was credited as an author of a research report. What a byline proves, and what happens to work without one.

CASE FILE 03 · PP. 65-66

Two Letters, One Man

On August 30, 1791, Thomas Jefferson called Benjamin Banneker "a very respectable Mathematician." On October 8, 1809, writing privately to Joel Barlow, he described "a mind of very common stature indeed." The proof had not changed. Read the two letters side by side.

CASE FILE 02 · PP. 50-51

Who Kept the Records

After John Brown's papers were seized in 1859, William Still hid his ledgers of people who escaped slavery in a building at Lebanon Cemetery. In 1872 they became his book, The Underground Rail Road. A talk about why some records survive, and who kept them.
